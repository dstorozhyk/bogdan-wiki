# Emergency fund liquidity tiers

## Purpose

An emergency fund is not only a target amount. It is a set of reserves with different **time-to-access**, failure domains and acceptable risk.

The tier model prevents two opposite mistakes:

- keeping the whole reserve instantly available but exposed to one bank/jurisdiction;
- chasing yield until funds are unavailable in the emergency they are meant to cover.

## The three tiers

### Tier 1 — Critical access

**Access objective:** hours.

Role:

- immediate living and emergency payments;
- first response during income interruption;
- instruments that do not require a broker sale, maturity or long withdrawal path.

The tier should survive the temporary loss of one payment rail. Accessibility matters more than return.

### Tier 2 — Operating buffer

**Access objective:** roughly 1–3 days.

Role:

- continue essential spending after Tier 1;
- hold instruments with modest settlement/redemption friction;
- diversify away from one bank or cash location without accepting long lock-up.

### Tier 3 — Strategic reserve

**Access objective:** roughly 1–2 weeks can be acceptable.

Role:

- protect the later months of runway;
- allow somewhat more operational friction or duration;
- diversify institutions and jurisdictions;
- remain capital-preservation oriented rather than become a growth portfolio.

## Denys's recorded target model

The approved strategy currently uses a target split of approximately:

- Tier 1: **17%**;
- Tier 2: **33%**;
- Tier 3: **50%**.

These are strategy targets, not evidence of current balances. The historical dashboard must be refreshed before any decision.

## Design dimensions

Evaluate the fund across more than asset labels:

1. **Time to access** — hours, days, weeks.
2. **Institution** — cash location, bank, broker, exchange/custodian.
3. **Jurisdiction** — Ukrainian and external operating perimeters.
4. **Asset/issuer risk** — cash, sovereign debt, fund/ETF, stablecoin issuer.
5. **Operational dependency** — card network, KYC, market hours, settlement, withdrawal.
6. **Concentration** — one platform or one failure mode should not disable the whole reserve.

## Use order

1. Use Tier 1 for immediate emergencies.
2. Use Tier 2 after Tier 1 is insufficient.
3. Use Tier 3 as the last reserve.
4. After any drawdown, restore the depleted earlier tier before resuming discretionary investing.

## Boundary with investing

The emergency fund optimizes **resilience and recoverability**, not maximum expected return. A higher yield is relevant only after liquidity, custody, jurisdiction and access risks remain acceptable.

Current instruments, rates, taxes, platform health and regulatory constraints are volatile and belong in dated research or live verification—not in this concept page.
